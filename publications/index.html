  1. Wake synchronization and cylinder forces near moving ridges

    Meysam Rajabi and Ersegun D. Gedikli (2026). Journal of Fluid Mechanics. Under submission

    Abstract

    We study the loading and wake dynamics of a stationary circular cylinder near periodically spaced ridges attached to a moving wall. The wall moves at the free-stream speed. Using two-dimensional simulations at Reynolds number Re = 100, we vary ridge spacing while keeping ridge shape and the cylinder–wall gap fixed. The ridge-passage frequency relative to the smooth-wall shedding frequency, F = fₚ/f₀, ranges from 0.8 to 1.2 across nine cases. We ask whether synchronization with ridge passage reduces force fluctuations. Ridge-relative phase and complete force waveforms identify two sampled synchronized cases, F = 0.95 and F = 1. Compared with the smooth-wall reference, root-mean-square lift fluctuations are approximately 50% lower in the first case and 45% higher in the second. Drag fluctuations increase in all nine cases. The synchronized response with lower lift fluctuations has a strong second harmonic, but its complete waveform repeats once per ridge passage. At the same ridge positions, the two synchronized cases show different shear-layer, reverse-flow, and pressure patterns despite having the same minimum clearance. Separate three-dimensional continuations of nonsynchronized two-dimensional wakes at Re = 200 and 300 show sustained span-dependent motion, changes in lift amplitude, and continuing phase drift over 80 ridge periods. In the late comparison window, the Re = 300 configuration has larger lift fluctuations but smaller mean span-dependent near-wake energy than the Re = 200 configuration. These results distinguish regular ridge passage, wake synchronization, and reduced cylinder loading. Assessing the response requires the force magnitude, waveform, and flow structure alongside the timing of ridge passage.

  2. Phase and recovery effects on cylinder wake response to traveling ridges

    Meysam Rajabi and Ersegun D. Gedikli (2026). Physics of Fluids. Under review

    Abstract

    This study uses two-dimensional numerical simulations to examine how a ridge traveling along a nearby wall affects the loading and wake of a circular cylinder. The Reynolds number based on cylinder diameter is 100, the gap-to-diameter ratio is G/D = 0.4, and the wall and ridge move at the free-stream speed. Single ridge passages are examined at three ridge heights and eight arrival phases. Ridge height sets the scale of the loading: pressure accounts for about 94–95% of the unfiltered peak drag increment. Arrival phase determines how positive and negative loading cancel and whether the subsequent vortex shedding advances or lags. A second ridge of fixed geometry is then introduced while the wake recovers from the first passage. Its additional effect is measured by comparison with cases that receive no second ridge. An empirical amplitude coordinate derived from the lift history describes the incoming recovery state. For four new cases with moderate departures from the reference cycle, a quadratic correction based on this amplitude reduces the root-mean-square error in late phase reset by about 43% compared with a prediction based on phase alone, without refitting. The correction performs worse than phase alone during earlier recovery, although the sampled outgoing near-wake responses remain similar between corresponding moderate and earlier-recovery cases. These results connect pressure-dominated loading to the later wake response and identify when recovery information improves predictions of successive ridge encounters.

  3. Wake-state-dependent cylinder loading during successive body passages

    Meysam Rajabi and Ersegun D. Gedikli (2026). Journal of Fluid Mechanics. Under review

    Abstract

    Three-dimensional immersed-boundary simulations at Re = 400 quantify the transient loading of a circular cylinder as finite-span square or triangular wall-attached bodies pass beneath it on a wall moving at the free-stream speed. Same-time subtraction from a matching smooth-wall continuation defines the event-conditioned pressure and force responses. The direct response to an isolated passage decreases by approximately 96% as the cylinder–body clearance increases from g/D = 0.1 to 2.0. In contrast, the delayed response varies non-monotonically and can exceed the direct maximum at intermediate clearances. For successive passages, a repeated leading-arrival kernel produces apparent shielding and amplification. A four-phase ensemble shows that this discrepancy is governed primarily by the cylinder-wake state when the following body arrives. Replacing the repeated second kernel with a phase-matched isolated response reduces the normalized pressure residual by 54–88% for square bodies and 84–90% for triangular bodies. The triangular-body response then agrees closely with the conditioned superposition, with no more than weak shielding, whereas the square bodies retain a larger phase-dependent residual. Pressure accounts for approximately 96–98% of the root-mean-square force perturbations during the representative nominal event. Synchronized flow fields link this loading to gap-flow restriction, displacement and roll-up of the wall-facing cylinder shear layer, and a pressure asymmetry that persists after both bodies have passed. Square bodies generate the stronger immediate three-dimensional response, whereas triangular bodies produce the stronger delayed response. Successive-passage responses are therefore controlled not only by body geometry and spacing, but also by the state of the cylinder wake at each arrival.
